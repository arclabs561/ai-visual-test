Hi, I’m Eugene 👋

A few years ago, I decided to switch to Figma. Learning by poking around is limiting and inefficient, so I looked for a short guide to quickly get at least as efficient as I was before. I found nothing but a few long video courses, which are not my preferred format. Instead, I began reading everything I could find on Figma and, well, poking around and experimenting.

In 2019, I started a regular newsletter so I can share things I learned about Figma. That research became a foundation for this book, as I recorded and organized the best tips and recommendations. Even if you’ve been using the app for a while, chances are you’ll learn a few things and save time in the future.

Look at the Table of Contents below. Every topic was thoroughly researched and explained in-depth:

  • Learn the power of shapes and Vector Networks.
  • Create resizable layouts that adapt to changes.
  • Construct intricate Components based on a few core principles.
  • Keep projects consistent by reusing Styles.
  • Use real data in your design mockups.
  • Export assets and avoid surprises with SVG.

Why learn Figma from me?

A few things prepared me for writing this book:

  • I write Figmalion, a regular newsletter about Figma. It sums up what happened in a world of Figma, so I read all new articles, follow Twitter threads, listen to podcasts, and watch videos before deciding if they should be included.

  • While preparing the newsletter, I collected recommendations and tricks for this book for a year. I read all Figma’s help articles and blog posts as well, just so you don’t have to.
  • I built two plugins for Figma — Typographer and Proper Title Case. Over five thousand people have used them so far.
  • I was an early adopter of both Sketch and Figma. First tried Figma during the invite-only beta in 2016, completely switched in 2018.
  • I’ve been designing and developing for 20 years. During my time at Wildbit, I worked on multiple successful products built for other developers and designers.
  • I love reading manuals. I read manuals to my car, camera, and dishwasher. Sadly, most apps now write help articles instead of a comprehensive manual. A mistake that should be fixed.
