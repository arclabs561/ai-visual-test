Decentralized archives for community media

We develop a peer to peer library for bottom-up archives to prevent free content from disappearing or getting lost on the internet.

Archipel will allow to create and share folders with media, documents, full websites and metadata in a secure peer to peer network. Archipel is built upon the Dat Project - thereby, everyone can create an archive because Archipel needs neither centralized platforms nor servers. It aims to provide tools for autonomy and self-determined publishing without reliance on corporate or centralized services.

Sharing and mirroring will be easy. Multiple copies provide performant access and prevent data loss. Built-in versioning and signatures give proofs of authorship. Hubs may provide an index for multiple archives and easy access for regular browsers.

Problems to be solved

The internet offers many ways to share and publish media, documents and other resource. Their long-term preservation and accessibility, however, is not easily guaranteed.

Usually, media is published on proprietary plattforms - which makes their future accessibility dependent on the platform's survival, business interest and open access policy.

Hosting a website you own is the common alternative. While this works well in many cases, especially for temporary projects like conferences, short-timed initiatives or working groups it happens frequently that after a certain while, a website and all hosted content goes offline again. Even big projects and online libraries fall under this fate, e.g. in case funding ends.

An autonomous society needs tools to not only publish content freely but also preserve and keep it accessible for the long term.

Technical details

Dat is an open source project to share folders securely and versioned in a p2p network. Archipel will extend Dat with tool for the easy creation, presentation and mirroring of multi-media archives with metadata.

  • archipel-app: A simple app to import websites, files and media with metadata into an archive: Paste a link to copy a website, pull in a Twitter or RSS feed and include linked media files, or upload files directly. The app can collect metadata and mirror the archive onto a Hub. It runs as a web app connected to a Hub, as an offline-first p2p app in Beaker Browser or as a desktop app.

  • archipel-hub: A webservice to mirror archives with a single click. It also provides an index and a web interface to browse the mirrored archives and view included contents and media. It will be based on Hashbase and datBase.

Many building blocks for these tools exist already (see awesome.datproject.org). We will develop Archipel with a focus on resuability and work within the Dat community.
